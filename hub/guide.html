Workbench · the rig

Set up the rig

From nothing to everything: the rig at home runs the AI, the Workbench Hub and holds all your music and videos. Your laptop and phone reach it through Tailscale, at home or away, and can keep songs for offline.

0 Start here

You need about two hours in total, most of it waiting for downloads and copying. Do the parts in order: each one needs the one before.

  • The rig (the computer that stays on), with the monitor, keyboard and mouse plugged in for now, and internet.
  • An account to sign in to Tailscale on every device (a Google account is easiest).
  • Your laptop and your phone nearby, and a USB cable for the phone.
  • Anything with music on it: old drives, USB sticks, the phone.
  • Paper or your phone's notes for three things you'll write down: the Hub address, your name and your PIN.
Windows without a product key works fine for all of this. You'll see "Activate Windows" in the corner and the Personalize settings (wallpaper, colours) are locked, but updates, apps, Tailscale, the Hub and the AI all work normally. Nothing in this guide needs activation.
Windows 10 or 11? Click Start → Settings → System → About and look at Edition. The steps are written for Windows 11; where Windows 10 is different, it's shown in brackets.
Tick each step as you go; your ticks are remembered on this device.

W Workbench as an app

About 3 minutes. Do this on the rig now, and later on the laptop (part F) and the phone (part G). A downloaded Workbench file (like Workbench_6_3_0.html) opened in Chrome can't be installed as an app: Chrome only installs apps from a website. The app comes from the Workbench website instead. It's the same Workbench, always the newest version, it opens without internet once installed, and it gets its own window and icon.

  1. Open Chrome. Click the address bar at the top, type tonystarkofficial1111-web.github.io/Workbench and press Enter. If it says 404, try it with a small w: tonystarkofficial1111-web.github.io/workbench.
  2. Wait until Workbench has fully opened. The first time it can take up to a minute: it's a big app. Click I understand if the notice shows.
  3. Install it. At the right end of the address bar, click the install icon (a small screen with a down arrow) → Install. If you don't see the icon: click ⋮ (top right of Chrome) → Cast, save, and share → Install page as app… → Install. (In older Chrome: ⋮ → Install Workbench….) In Edge: ⋯ → Apps → Install this site as an app → Install.
  4. Workbench opens in its own window, without Chrome's tabs and address bar. If Chrome asks whether to pin it to the taskbar or Start, say Yes. Otherwise right-click its icon on the taskbar → Pin to taskbar.
  5. From now on, open Workbench from that icon (or Start → type Workbench). You don't need the downloaded file any more; keep it as a spare or delete it. Anything you did in the downloaded file stays in that file's copy. On the rig that's nothing yet, so there's nothing to move. On a device where you already have Work in the file, connect the Hub from the file first (part F, step 3), then open the app and connect it too: everything comes over from the rig.
  6. The app updates itself. Whenever it's online it quietly downloads the newest version, and you get it the next time you open the app (close it and open it again to get it straight away). The version number is in the bottom right corner.

A Prepare the rig

About 20 minutes. Do everything on the rig with the monitor, keyboard and mouse plugged in. Plug it into the router with a network cable if you can; it is faster and steadier than Wi-Fi.

  1. Sign in to Windows on the rig with the account you will leave it on. Everything below is installed for that account.
  2. Update Windows. Click Start → Settings (the gear) → Windows Update (left side, at the bottom) → Check for updates. (Windows 10: Settings → Update & Security → Windows Update.) Let it download and install everything, and click Restart now when it asks. After the restart, go back and press Check for updates again. Repeat until it says You're up to date.
  3. Update the graphics driver. Click Start, type NVIDIA and open the NVIDIA App (or GeForce Experience). Go to Drivers → Download → Express installation. If neither is installed: open a browser, go to nvidia.com/drivers, choose GeForce, your card's series and model, and your Windows version, then Find → Download, and run the file with the default choices. Restart the rig when it's done. The AI needs this to use the graphics card.
  4. Let it sign back in after updates. Settings → Accounts → Sign-in options → scroll down → turn on Use my sign-in info to automatically finish setting up after an update. (Windows 10: it's under Privacy on the same page, called Use my sign-in info to automatically finish setting up my device after an update or restart.) If you don't see it, skip it. The Hub starts when Windows signs in, so this keeps it running after update restarts.
  5. Never sleep. Settings → System → Power & battery (or Power) → Screen and sleep → set When plugged in, put my device to sleep after to Never. (Windows 10: Settings → System → Power & sleep → under Sleep, When plugged in, PC goes to sleep after → Never.) The screen turning off is fine. (The Hub installer also does this.)
  6. Optional: turn back on after a power cut. Restart the rig and, as soon as it starts, tap Del (or F2) until the BIOS opens. Look under Advanced → APM Configuration (or Power) for Restore AC Power Loss and set it to Power On. Press F10 → Yes to save. Names differ a little between boards; skip this if you can't find it.

B Tailscale on the rig

About 10 minutes. Tailscale links your own devices privately, so your laptop and phone reach the rig from anywhere, at home or away. Nothing is opened to the internet, and it is free. Do this before the Hub installer: the installer uses it.

  1. On the rig, open a browser, go to tailscale.com/download and click Download Tailscale for Windows. Open the downloaded file and click Install. If Windows asks Do you want to allow this app to make changes, click Yes.
  2. A Tailscale icon appears at the bottom right, near the clock (click the ^ arrow if you don't see it). Click it → Log in. A browser page opens: sign in with the account you'll use on every device, for example your Google account. Click Connect if it asks. The icon now shows you're connected.
  3. Name the rig. In the browser go to login.tailscale.com/admin/machines. Find the rig in the list → click the ⋯ at the end of its row → Edit machine name… → turn off Auto-generate from OS hostname if it's there → type rig → Update name.
  4. Turn on secure addresses. Go to login.tailscale.com/admin/dns. Check that MagicDNS is on (click Enable MagicDNS if you see it). Scroll down to HTTPS Certificates → Enable HTTPS → confirm. Write down the name shown on that page (it looks like tail1234.ts.net).

C Make your big folder and fill it

As long as it takes to copy your files. Put all your music and videos into one folder on the rig. Workbench reads it where it is: nothing is moved or copied again, and anything you add later shows up by itself.

Inside Everything you can organise however you like (by artist, album, playlist…). Keep songs inside a folder named Music (or Songs, Audio, Beats, DJ) and videos inside Videos (or Movies, Clips). Folders named Photos, Pictures, Camera, DCIM or Screenshots are skipped, and so are programs and tiny sound effects.
  1. Pick the drive. Press Win + E to open File Explorer and click This PC on the left. Each drive shows how much space is free. Use the one with the most free space (often D:; use C: if that's the only one).
  2. Make the folder. Double-click that drive. Right-click an empty spot → New → Folder, type Everything and press Enter. Double-click Everything, then make two folders inside it the same way: Music and Videos. You now have, for example, D:\Everything\Music and D:\Everything\Videos.
  3. From an old drive or a USB stick: plug it into the rig. It appears under This PC. Open it, open the folder with your songs, press Ctrl + A to select everything, then Ctrl + C. Open Everything\Music and press Ctrl + V. Wait for the copy window to finish. Do the same with videos into Everything\Videos.
  4. From an Android phone with a USB cable: unlock the phone and plug it into the rig. On the phone, pull down from the top and tap the Charging this device via USB notice → choose File transfer (or Transferring files). On the rig, in File Explorer under This PC, open the phone → Internal storage → open Music (also look in Download). Select the songs, copy them, and paste them into Everything\Music. If the phone doesn't appear, try another cable: some cables only charge.
  5. From the laptop: copy the songs onto a USB stick and do step 3, or wait until part F, where you can send them over from the laptop instead.
  6. Check the formats. These play everywhere: MP3, M4A/AAC, FLAC, WAV, OGG, Opus (music) and MP4, WEBM, MOV, MKV (videos). WMA files don't play in browsers. To convert them, install VLC (videolan.org), then Media → Convert / Save → Add the files → Convert / Save → Profile Audio - MP3 → Start. Songs bought or downloaded inside Spotify or Apple Music are locked to those apps and can't be copied.
  7. Copy the folder's path for later. Open Everything, click the address bar at the top of the window so it shows the full path (like D:\Everything), and write it down. You'll type or paste it in the installer.

D Install the Hub and the AI

About 20 minutes, mostly downloading. One installer does it all: the Hub, starting it with Windows, its tray icon, the AI (Ollama) and the AI model. A black window asks questions; type the answer and press Enter each time.

On the rig you don't need the OLLAMA_ORIGINS or OLLAMA_HOST settings. The Hub talks to the AI on the rig itself and shares it with your devices.
  1. On the rig, open a browser and go to tonystarkofficial1111-web.github.io/Workbench/hub/WorkbenchHub.zip. It downloads to your Downloads folder.
  2. Open Downloads (Win + E → Downloads on the left) → right-click WorkbenchHub.zip → Extract All… → Extract. A new folder opens.
  3. Open the WorkbenchHub folder inside it and double-click Install Workbench Hub. If a blue box says Windows protected your PC, click More info → Run anyway. If it asks to allow changes, click Yes.
  4. 1/7 Node.js and 2/7 The Hub program install by themselves. Just wait.
  5. 3/7 Where to keep everything: it lists your drives with their free space and suggests a folder in brackets. Press Enter to accept it, or type one like D:\Workbench Hub and press Enter. This is the Hub's own folder (Work, design files, saves, and songs you send it later); it's different from your big folder.
  6. Your big folder: it asks Got a big folder of your own stuff? Type the path from part C, step 7 (like D:\Everything) and press Enter. To paste instead, right-click inside the black window. It should say Workbench will read D:\Everything.
  7. 4/7 You, the owner: type your name and press Enter. Then choose a PIN of 4 to 12 digits, press Enter, and type it again. Nothing shows while you type the PIN; that's normal. Write the name and PIN down: you sign in with them on every device.
  8. 5/7 Start with Windows is done for you.
  9. 6/7 Tailscale: it shares the Hub with your own devices. If Tailscale shows a link saying HTTPS or Serve isn't enabled, open that link, click Enable, and the installer carries on by itself. Then it prints Your Hub's address, like https://rig.tail1234.ts.net, and copies it. Write this address down.
  10. 7/7 The AI: it asks Install Ollama now…? (Y/N). Type Y → Enter. Ollama downloads (about 1 GB) and installs; a llama icon appears near the clock. Then it asks Download the AI model llama3.1:8b…? (Y/N). Type Y → Enter. It's about 5 GB; a progress bar shows.
  11. At the end it says THE HUB IS RUNNING with your folder and address. Press any key to close. Near the clock there's now a Workbench Hub icon; hover over it and it says Workbench Hub - Online.

E Check the AI uses the graphics card

About 5 minutes.

  1. Click Start, type PowerShell and open Windows PowerShell.
  2. Type ollama run llama3.1:8b and press Enter. Wait for >>> (10 to 30 seconds the first time). Type Say hi in Spanish → Enter. It answers.
  3. Type /bye → Enter. Then type ollama ps → Enter. Under PROCESSOR it should say 100% GPU. If it says CPU, update the graphics driver (part A, step 3), restart the rig and check again.
  4. If the installer couldn't install Ollama: go to ollama.com/download → Download for Windows → run OllamaSetup.exe → Install. Then in PowerShell run ollama pull llama3.1:8b and do steps 2 and 3 again.

F The laptop

About 15 minutes.

  1. On the laptop go to tailscale.com/download → install it the same way as on the rig → click its icon → Log in with the same account as the rig.
  2. Open Workbench. If you already have Work in the copy you've been using on the laptop (a downloaded file), open that one for this first connection so your Work goes up to the rig. Then install the app as in part W and connect it too (step 3 again); from then on just use the app.
  3. Click the bell at the top right → Connect the Hub. Fill in Hub address (from part D, like https://rig.tail1234.ts.net), Your name and PIN, and leave This device as it is (or type Laptop). Click Connect. If the browser asks to let the site reach devices on your network, click Allow. It says Connected, and your Work, design files and saves go up to the rig.
  4. In the same window, under AI, click Use the rig's AI here.
  5. Check your big folder. Scroll down to Folders on the rig. Your Everything folder is there with how many songs and videos it found (give it a minute). If it's not there, click Add a folder…, click through the rig's drives to Everything → Use this folder. Leave Family can see it off unless it's for everyone.
  6. Send the laptop's own songs to the rig. Click Send music to the rig (in the Hub window under Music and videos), or go to Media → Rig library → Send to the rig. Pick the songs: click the first, hold Shift and click the last to pick many, or press Ctrl + A for the whole folder → Open. A bar shows each song going up. They land in your Music on the rig.
  7. Try the AI: open Work → the round chat button at the bottom right → ask What should I do today?
  8. Only if you installed Ollama on the laptop before: Settings → Apps → Installed apps (Windows 10: Apps & features) → Ollama → ⋯ → Uninstall. Then in File Explorer go to C:\Users\your name and delete the .ollama folder (several GB). Click Start, type environment variables → Edit environment variables for your account → select OLLAMA_ORIGINS → Delete, the same for OLLAMA_HOST → OK.

G The phone

About 10 minutes.

  1. Open the Play Store, search Tailscale → Install → Open → Get started / Log in with the same account. When Android asks to set up a VPN connection, tap OK. Make sure the switch says Connected.
  2. Install Workbench as an app. In Chrome open tonystarkofficial1111-web.github.io/Workbench, wait until it's fully open → tap ⋮ (top right) → Add to Home screen (or Install app) → Install. Open it from the new icon on your home screen.
  3. Tap the bell → Connect the Hub → type the Hub address, Your name and PIN, and Phone as This device → Connect. Your Work and saves come over.
  4. Under Notifications on this device → Turn on → Allow. Then Send a test; a notification arrives within seconds. If not: Android Settings → Apps → Workbench → Notifications on, and Battery → Unrestricted.
  5. Under AI → Use the rig's AI here.
  6. Songs that are only on the phone: Media → Rig library → Send to the rig → pick them (press and hold one, then tap the others to pick several) → Select. Or copy them with a cable as in part C, step 4.

H Your music on every device

The same on the laptop and the phone.

  1. Open Media → Rig library (in the row of sections under the title) → Music. Every song on the rig is listed: your big folder, everything you've sent, and links you've saved. Use Search the rig… to find one.
  2. Tap ▷ on a song to play it from the rig, or Play all. It streams over Tailscale, at home or away.
  3. For offline (a flight, school, no signal): tap Keep on a song; it saves on that device and shows ✓ Here. Keep all here saves everything; check the phone has room first (about 5 MB a song, so 1,000 songs ≈ 5 GB).
  4. Adding more later: copy new files into Everything\Music on the rig, or use Send to the rig from any device. They appear within a minute or so.
  5. The DJ sees them too: Work → DJ → Library → Rig library.
  6. Spotify Wrapped: only songs Spotify itself plays count. Next to a song, the green Spotify button pauses it here and opens it in Spotify (the app if you have it), so playing it there counts. It only finds songs that are on Spotify.

I Leave it running

The Hub updates itself, keeps a daily copy of your Work (90 days, in the Hub folder's hidden .hub\backups), and writes the AI's hourly summary on the graphics card.
  1. Leave the rig on and unplug the monitor if you like. Everything keeps running.
  2. On your phone, the small dot on the bell is the rig: green = online. In the bell → Hub it says Online · rig.
  3. Family: on the laptop, bell → Hub → People → type their name and a PIN → Add person. On their device they install Tailscale and sign in with your account (or, in Tailscale's admin page → Machines → rig → ⋯ → Share…, share the rig with their own account), then connect the Hub with their name and PIN. They see only their own music unless you tick Family can see it on a folder.
